# How the layout rules measure

What the rules that read a rendered page measure, and where they stop. [`LIMITATIONS.md`](./LIMITATIONS.md) gives the short version of each limit; this is the detail, for checking a finding. All of it applies in a real browser; jsdom has no layout.

## Skipped content

`content-visibility: auto` lets the browser skip laying out the content of an element that is off screen: until it nears the viewport, the element keeps the size `contain-intrinsic-size` gives it (or the size it last had), and its content, which script can still measure, is laid out spilling out of a box of that size, over whatever follows. No reader sees that, since the browser lays the element out at its real size once it nears the viewport. So for the scan, every element with `content-visibility: auto` is laid out as it is near the viewport: `content-visibility: visible`, with the layout, style and paint containment `auto` gives it added to its own `contain`, set in its style attribute with `!important`. An element already near the viewport is measured as before, and one whose content was skipped is measured at its real size, by every rule that reads the layout: text spacing, overlap and clipping, contrast backgrounds, focus visibility and target size. Measured as skipped, a card longer than its placeholder made its text overlap the next card's under the text spacing, its text sit over the next box's background, its targets crowd the next card's, and its last button lie past the end of the page. When the scan ends, each style attribute is set back to its own text, or taken off where there was none, and the browser skips the content again from its next frame; the scan is one synchronous task, so the browser never paints the change. `engine.environment.contentVisibilitySkipped` says how many elements had their content skipped. Limits: elements in closed shadow roots are not reached, and content a page's script adds only once an element comes into view (an `IntersectionObserver` that loads it) isn't there to measure. `content-visibility: hidden` is not changed: its content is hidden from every reader, and the rules leave it out.

## Contrast

In a browser, the contrast rules measure text against what is painted under it, in CSS's painting order. Text positioned over a sibling, over an `<img>` hero, over a `::before` overlay, or pulled over another block by a negative margin sits on paint none of its ancestors own, and text that overflows an ancestor's box, or has a negative `z-index`, isn't over that ancestor's background. In a browser the engine measures the text's line boxes, finds the elements that paint there (a background color or image, replaced content such as `img`, `video`, `canvas`, `iframe`, SVG shapes), and orders them with the text and its ancestors' backgrounds by the CSS painting order: stacking contexts, `z-index`, positioned boxes and floats, then document order. When everything between the text and the first opaque layer is a solid color covering all of the text, the ratio is measured against that; when an image, a gradient, paint covering only part of the text, or paint drawn over the text is involved, `contrast-computable` asks about the text with reason code `BACKGROUND_OVERLAP` and the ratio rules leave it out. A `::before` or `::after` positioned absolutely against its element takes part in that order too, by its `z-index`: with a negative one it is painted over its stacking context's own background and under its text (the hover fill of a button, a card's overlay), as a child of the text's element with a negative `z-index` is, and it is under text that has a stacking context of its own above it. Under the text, its solid color is measured against and an image or gradient is asked about; over the text, it is in the way, unless it is an opaque color the same as what lies under the text (a white fade-out over white). It is placed by its offsets, margins and border box; one whose paint isn't known, with a `clip-path`, a `clip` or a mask, or under a scale, rotation or zoom on it or its element's ancestors, is asked about, and so is text over its border or over the part its `background-clip` leaves bare. A fixed `::before` or `::after` with a color or gradient over the text is asked about. An SVG `<rect>` drawn with a stroke and no fill, such as a chart's frame, paints only its outline: text inside it, clear of the stroke and its rounded corners (a percentage radius is of the SVG viewport), is not on it; a stroke width or radius that can't be read counts the whole box. Other shapes count by their box, and an inline `<svg>` an HTML text lies over counts as replaced content. A `background-image` whose layers are all `none` paints no image: a computed style lists one value per background layer, so an element given two layers and no image reads `none, none`. A gradient whose stops are all one color is that color (a stop in a color it can't read makes it a gradient), and a background layer that paints nothing under the text is left out: one sized to nothing, one or two 2px thick or less along an axis they don't repeat on (an underline, as the DSFR draws its links; more such layers are asked about), or, in a browser, a solid layer placed clear of the text's line boxes. A layer fixed to the viewport (`background-attachment: fixed`) counts only when it tiles both ways, and one repeated with `space` only when a tile is the size of the box; a solid layer covering part of a box that is scaled, rotated or zoomed, or scrolling with a scroll container's content, is asked about. A solid layer covering all of the text is measured as a color over the background color, and an opaque one hides the layers under it; one covering only part of the text is asked about (`BACKGROUND_IMAGE_OR_GRADIENT`), as text over an inline box's partial layer is, since the text is not measured against each of the colors it sits on. Under jsdom, which keeps only the first value of a longhand listing several layers, a background with more than one layer that isn't `none` is asked about. Text that a box clipping its overflow keeps partly or wholly out of view, such as the options further down a scrolled list or a slide outside a carousel's track, is measured as it is once scrolled into that box: against the box and its ancestors, and paint inside the box, not against what lies where it is now, outside the box. One exception: text a box with `overflow: hidden` (or `clip`) keeps wholly out of view, such as a carousel slide off its track or an option of a menu scrolled by a custom scrollbar, is not measured at all. From its styles alone, such a box can't be told from one that hides that text for good (a teaser cut at a fixed height, a collapsed panel), so the text is left out rather than guessed at. It is measured once in view: scan the page in that state (the slide shown, the menu scrolled) to check it. In a box that scrolls with `overflow: auto` or `scroll`, it is always measured. Limits: a fixed or sticky box over the text is left out, since it covers it only at the scroll position the scan was taken at (one behind the text counts); a gradient under the text is not sampled, so faint text on a faint gradient is asked about rather than failed; and a group effect (`opacity`, a filter, a blend mode) on an ancestor leaves the overlap to the older, coarser check. A filter that is only `drop-shadow()` is no such effect, and doesn't stop the measurement, where the element or a box inside it paints an opaque background behind all of the text's line boxes, clear of its rounded corners, as a page header's does: the shadow is drawn under the element's content and hidden where that is opaque. Text that overflows that box lies on the shadow and is asked about, as is all such text under jsdom, which has no line boxes. Text with no opaque background behind it up to the root is measured on the canvas the browser paints under the page, in either contrast mode. The canvas follows from the page's own `color-scheme` (on the root, or in `<meta name="color-scheme">`), as CSS Color Adjustment defines it, so nothing is guessed: a page that declares none is drawn in light, on white, whatever the user prefers; one that declares `dark` on the dark canvas (`#121212` in Chromium); one that declares `light dark` in the scheme the browser prefers, and in the other too where the text's colors can't change with the scheme (no `prefers-color-scheme` rule, `light-dark()` or system color reaches them). Text that fails in one scheme only names it in `details.colorScheme`; where a scheme couldn't be measured, the finding and the pass list those that were in `details.colorSchemesChecked`. In a frame, whose canvas lets the parent page show through when the two share a color scheme, the canvas is not read, and the text is asked about (`BACKGROUND_NOT_OPAQUE_AT_ROOT`), or in `auditorAssist` mode measured on `contrast.rootCanvasFallback`. A `contrast.rootCanvasFallback` the scan names is measured in place of the page's canvas, in either mode (`ROOT_CANVAS_FALLBACK`). Under jsdom there is no layout, so text is measured against its ancestors alone, and on the canvas as a browser would paint it, with Chromium's colors (`#121212` canvas, `#ffffff` text and `#9e9eff` links in dark; light where a page supports both).

Text is held to the ratio for the size it is drawn at, which decides whether it is large text: its `font-size` scaled by CSS `zoom` (not the user's browser zoom), by an SVG's viewBox and transforms, and by `font-size-adjust`, which draws a font at its `font-size` times the adjust value over the font's own aspect (x-height by default), read from a probe in the same font. A finding drawn at another size than its `font-size` gives `typography.declaredFontSizePx` and `typography.sizeScaledBy`. Under jsdom, which lays nothing out and reads no font, the `font-size` is taken as it is. Text a block moves off the page with a negative `text-indent` (`text-indent: -9999px`, an image-replacement technique), its own or generated by `::before`, is drawn nowhere and is left out, as text positioned off the page is; the lines after an indented first line, and an inline box, where `text-indent` moves nothing, are still measured.

Generated text is measured too, in a browser: the text a `::before` or `::after` `content` writes, and the marker of a list item (`<li>`). It is read from the pseudo-element's computed style: strings as written, `attr()` as the browser resolves it, a counter (and a marker's number) as letters or digits in its list style, and a marker's own `content` or a `list-style-type` string. Quotation marks, bullets, icon-font glyphs with no letter or digit, and images (`content: url(...)`, `list-style-image`) are not text, as elsewhere. It is measured in the pseudo-element's color and font, so a large `::before` gets the large-text ratio, against its element's background and, over it, the pseudo-element's own background color; an outside marker of an item that paints a background of its own is measured against the item's parent, since it is drawn outside the item's box. The result points at the element, with `generatedBy` naming the pseudo-element, and the summary says it is the text the pseudo-element generates. Asked about (`contrast-computable`) rather than measured: content that can't be read, such as a counter in a page's own `@counter-style` (`GENERATED_TEXT_UNREADABLE`); a `::before` or `::after` positioned absolutely or fixed with no opaque background of its own, which can sit over anything (`GENERATED_TEXT_POSITIONED`); a shadow, an outline, a blend mode, a filter or a background image on it; a background or opacity of its own under an `opacity`; and whatever stops its element's own text being measured. Limits: `::before` and `::after` are looked for only on the elements a style sheet's selector naming one matches (all elements where a sheet can't be read); markers only on `<li>`, not on other elements made list items; and under jsdom, which computes no pseudo-element styles, generated text is left out.

### Outlined text

Text whose fill is transparent, or the color of its background, can still be drawn by a `-webkit-text-stroke` or a `text-shadow`. Under ACT's definition such text is visible, and WCAG's techniques count an outline or halo in a text's contrast (G18, G145), so the rules measure it rather than skip it. Against a flat, opaque background, the text is measured by each color it is drawn in, with that color's alpha and the element's opacity. A fill that is seen counts among those colors, so a light grey fill with a light grey outline on white is measured too. When even the best of them is below the ratio, `contrast-minimum` and `contrast-enhanced` fail it, each at its own ratio, and the details say what draws it (`drawnBy: 'textStroke'` or `'textShadow'`; a stroke wins when there are both). `contrast-computable` doesn't also ask about text below the AA ratio. Text whose fill or outline reaches the ratio, text over an image or a gradient, a stroke or shadow color that can't be read, and a placeholder whose fill is seen are asked about instead: `contrast-computable` `cantTell` with `TEXT_STROKE` or `TEXT_SHADOW`, since how legible an outline is takes a person. Text nothing draws, with no stroke or shadow or only transparent ones, is skipped. The helpers are `contrast.getOutlineColors` and `contrast.measureOutlineOnlyText`. jsdom has no `-webkit-text-stroke`, so there only shadows apply.

### Forced colors

Under forced colors (Windows high contrast, or Chromium's emulated `forced-colors: active`) the browser paints the user's colors over the page's. WCAG 1.4.3 and 1.4.6 are about the page's own colors, and forced colors are the reader's setting, so the rules measure the page's own colors whatever the setting: a page scanned with forced colors on gives the same outcomes, ratios and occurrences as with them off, unless its own CSS reacts to them. A `@media (forced-colors: active)` rule still matches while the scan runs, and a system color (`GrayText`, `Canvas`, `ButtonText`…) still takes the forced palette's value, so text styled that way is measured in colors the page has only under forced colors: a badge the page restyles to `Canvas` and `CanvasText` there passes, though it fails with forced colors off. The scan says so in `engine.environment.forcedColorsCss: true`; check such a page again with forced colors off. Measured as the browser leaves them, they would be neither the page's colors nor the ones on screen: Chromium forces `color` and `background-color` in the computed style but not the text fill color the rules read, so dark gray text on the page's black background, which fails, would be measured on white and pass, a link told apart by color alone would be measured in the page's link color on the forced background, and text on a gradient, which the browser drops, would be measured on the plain background rather than asked about. So for the scan the engine keeps the page out of forced colors: a style sheet giving every element `forced-color-adjust: none !important`, adopted by the document and by each open shadow root (whose own sheets could set it back), and inherited by pseudo-elements and closed shadow roots. Where a document can't adopt a style sheet, a `<style>` at the start of its head does it. The scan takes out exactly what it added when it ends, and leaves the page's own styles alone, its own `forced-color-adjust` included. The scan is one synchronous task, so the browser never paints the page in its own colors, as with animations held still. Each frame does this for itself when it is scanned. The canvas behind a page that paints no background of its own is the exception: under forced colors the browser resolves the `Canvas` color, and the page's color scheme, to the forced palette (a page that asks for dark reads white), so the page's own canvas can't be read, and text on it is asked about (`contrast-computable`) rather than measured, as under jsdom; `auditorAssist` measures it on `contrast.rootCanvasFallback`, as an assumption it reports. `engine.environment.forcedColors` is `"active"` all the same, since the page was shown in forced colors. Where the page can't be kept in its own colors (a Content Security Policy blocks the `<style>` and the browser can't adopt a style sheet), the colors measured are forced, and every occurrence of `contrast-minimum`, `contrast-enhanced` and `contrast-computable` carries `details.forcedColors: true` and a hint (`contrast_hint_forcedColors`) saying to check the page again with forced colors off. Under jsdom there is no media query to ask, so nothing is done, the field is absent and no occurrence is marked.

## Form fields

A form field's text is measured as the field draws it. The value of an `<input>` that shows text (text, search, email, url, tel, password, number, the date and time types) and the current value of a `<textarea>` are measured in the field's color against its background; while the value is empty, the placeholder is measured in the `::placeholder` color and font, faded by its `opacity`, over its own background if it has one. A placeholder with a background image or gradient, a shadow, or `opacity` over a background of its own is asked about (`contrast-computable`) rather than measured. A drop-down `<select>` (no `multiple`, a `size` of 1 or none) is measured as the closed control draws it: its shown option, the selected one, in the select's own color, font and background, whatever colors the option has; the result points at the select. When the page left the select's background to the browser, browsers draw it differently: Chromium paints the field in the `Field` system color (white), though its computed background is `ButtonFace` (`#efefef`), which other browsers may paint. Text that fails on either fails, so the text is measured against both and the lower ratio counts; the finding's `selectField` gives both colors (`backgroundHex`, `fieldHex`). Its other options are shown only in the list it opens, which the platform draws, in the options' colors or in its own: an option with the select's colors counts as the select's text, and one with a color, a background or an `opacity` of its own is asked about (`SELECT_OPTION_COLOR`) rather than measured. A list box (`multiple`, or a `size` above 1) draws its options, and a customizable select (`appearance: base-select`) draws its list as the page styles it, so their options are measured in their own colors, and the button of a customizable select by its own text; a customizable select with no `<button>` of the page's own shows its selected option in the closed control, measured in the select's colors as a drop-down's is. Limits: the text the browser draws when a date or time field is empty (`mm/dd/yyyy`), and a file input's button and file name, are not measured; text in the field is measured against the field and its ancestors, not against other paint overlapping the field. Under jsdom there is no `::placeholder` style, so placeholders are not measured there; values are. A placeholder below its ratio (`PLACEHOLDER_BELOW_THRESHOLD`) fails when the page chose its colors: the `::placeholder` color, opacity or background, or the field's background, differ from the browser's own, which a scan reads from the same kind of field in a blank frame. WCAG holds placeholder text to the ratio (Understanding 1.4.3), but counts colors the page left entirely to the browser as meeting it (technique G148), and Chromium's own #757575 is 4.61:1 on white, under AAA's 7:1; so in the browser's own colors a placeholder passes, and where whose colors they are can't be told, it is a `cantTell`.

## Target size

`target-size-minimum` measures the region a pointer can hit, worked out from the layout. A target is its border box with its rounded corners and 2D transforms, clipped by `clip` and `clip-path: inset()` on its containing-block chain and no larger than a box there that clips its `overflow`, with content sticking out of it, less the boxes painted over it that take pointer events, ordered as the contrast rules order paint; it is large enough when a 24 by 24 square aligned to the page fits inside (Understanding 2.5.8). Because the region comes from the layout rather than from hit-testing, targets below the fold are measured as those in view. So are targets in a box that clips its overflow, such as a menu longer than its panel, whether it scrolls with `overflow: auto` or `scroll`, or with `overflow: hidden` and a script (a custom scrollbar, a virtual list): one past the box's edge, or wholly out of view, is measured whole, as scrolled into it, and only the boxes and targets inside the box count as covering it or near it there. Which part of a target shows at the scan's scroll position is never measured. A clipping box only bounds a target's size by its own: a 40px button in a 10px box is measured as 10px. So a target a box half hides for good, with no way to scroll it into view, is measured whole. Rounded corners are measured as drawn; Chromium accepts a pointer about a pixel beyond the curve, so a target measured as a hair under 24 can take a 24 by 24 pointer in that browser. Fixed and sticky boxes over a target are left out, as they cover it at one scroll position only; a target before the start of the page, where scrolling can't reach (a skip link at `left: -9999px`), is no target. Left as their bounding box: 3D transforms, other `clip-path` shapes, and an ancestor's rounded clipping. A square has to fit in one piece of the region (the box, one line of an inline link, one child sticking out), not across two that touch. Its inline exception covers a target in a sentence, a link or a button written into the text, which it reads as text with a letter or a digit outside any target in the stretch of the target's block between line breaks; whether such text is a sentence isn't judged, and links between separators alone ("Edit | Delete") that crowd each other are asked about rather than exempted. Under jsdom, which has no layout, the rule is `notApplicable` as before.

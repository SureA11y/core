# How the layout rules measure

What the rules that read a rendered page measure, and where they stop. [`LIMITATIONS.md`](./LIMITATIONS.md) gives the short version of each limit; this is the detail, for checking a finding or comparing results with another engine. All of it applies in a real browser; jsdom has no layout.

## Contrast

In a browser, the contrast rules measure text against what is painted under it, in CSS's painting order. Text positioned over a sibling, over an `<img>` hero, over a `::before` overlay, or pulled over another block by a negative margin sits on paint none of its ancestors own, and text that overflows an ancestor's box, or has a negative `z-index`, isn't over that ancestor's background. In a browser the engine measures the text's line boxes, finds the elements that paint there (a background color or image, replaced content such as `img`, `video`, `canvas`, `iframe`, SVG shapes), and orders them with the text and its ancestors' backgrounds by the CSS painting order: stacking contexts, `z-index`, positioned boxes and floats, then document order. When everything between the text and the first opaque layer is a solid color covering all of the text, the ratio is measured against that; when an image, a gradient, paint covering only part of the text, or paint drawn over the text is involved, `contrast-computable` asks about the text with reason code `BACKGROUND_OVERLAP` and the ratio rules leave it out, as they do for a positioned `::before` or `::after` with a color or gradient. Text that a box clipping its overflow keeps partly or wholly out of view, such as the options further down a scrolled list or a slide outside a carousel's track, is measured as it is once scrolled into that box: against the box and its ancestors, and paint inside the box, not against what lies where it is now, outside the box. One exception: text a box with `overflow: hidden` (or `clip`) keeps wholly out of view, such as a carousel slide off its track or an option of a menu scrolled by a custom scrollbar, is not measured at all. From its styles alone, such a box can't be told from one that hides that text for good (a teaser cut at a fixed height, a collapsed panel), so the text is left out rather than guessed at, as other engines leave it out. It is measured once in view: scan the page in that state (the slide shown, the menu scrolled) to check it. In a box that scrolls with `overflow: auto` or `scroll`, it is always measured. Limits: a fixed or sticky box over the text is left out, since it covers it only at the scroll position the scan was taken at (one behind the text counts); a gradient under the text is not sampled, so faint text on a faint gradient is asked about rather than failed; and a group effect (`opacity`, a filter, a blend mode) on an ancestor leaves the overlap to the older, coarser check. Text with no opaque background behind it up to the root is measured on the canvas the browser paints under the page, read from the page once per scan: white, or the dark canvas (`#121212` in Chromium) for a page that asks for a dark color scheme. It is measured so in either contrast mode, since the colour is read rather than assumed. In a frame, whose canvas lets the parent page show through when the two share a color scheme, it is not read. Under jsdom there is no layout, so text is measured against its ancestors alone, and text with no opaque background is asked about (`BACKGROUND_NOT_OPAQUE_AT_ROOT`), or in `auditorAssist` mode measured on `contrast.rootCanvasFallback`.

Generated text is measured too, in a browser: the text a `::before` or `::after` `content` writes, and the marker of a list item (`<li>`). It is read from the pseudo-element's computed style: strings as written, `attr()` as the browser resolves it, a counter (and a marker's number) as letters or digits in its list style, and a marker's own `content` or a `list-style-type` string. Quotation marks, bullets, icon-font glyphs with no letter or digit, and images (`content: url(...)`, `list-style-image`) are not text, as elsewhere. It is measured in the pseudo-element's color and font, so a large `::before` gets the large-text ratio, against its element's background and, over it, the pseudo-element's own background color; an outside marker of an item that paints a background of its own is measured against the item's parent, since it is drawn outside the item's box. The result points at the element, with `generatedBy` naming the pseudo-element, and the summary says it is the text the pseudo-element generates. Asked about (`contrast-computable`) rather than measured: content that can't be read, such as a counter in a page's own `@counter-style` (`GENERATED_TEXT_UNREADABLE`); a `::before` or `::after` positioned absolutely or fixed with no opaque background of its own, which can sit over anything (`GENERATED_TEXT_POSITIONED`); a shadow, an outline, a blend mode, a filter or a background image on it; a background or opacity of its own under an `opacity`; and whatever stops its element's own text being measured. Limits: `::before` and `::after` are looked for only on the elements a style sheet's selector naming one matches (all elements where a sheet can't be read); markers only on `<li>`, not on other elements made list items; and under jsdom, which computes no pseudo-element styles, generated text is left out.

## Form fields

A form field's text is measured as the field draws it. The value of an `<input>` that shows text (text, search, email, url, tel, password, number, the date and time types) and the current value of a `<textarea>` are measured in the field's color against its background; while the value is empty, the placeholder is measured in the `::placeholder` color and font, faded by its `opacity`, over its own background if it has one. A placeholder with a background image or gradient, a shadow, or `opacity` over a background of its own is asked about (`contrast-computable`) rather than measured. A drop-down `<select>` (no `multiple`, a `size` of 1 or none) is measured as the closed control draws it: its shown option, the selected one, in the select's own color, font and background, whatever colors the option has; the result points at the select. Its other options are shown only in the list it opens, which the platform draws, in the options' colors or in its own: an option with the select's colors counts as the select's text, and one with a color, a background or an `opacity` of its own is asked about (`SELECT_OPTION_COLOR`) rather than measured. A list box (`multiple`, or a `size` above 1) draws its options, and a customizable select (`appearance: base-select`) draws its list as the page styles it, so their options are measured in their own colors, and the button of a customizable select by its own text. Limits: the text the browser draws when a date or time field is empty (`mm/dd/yyyy`), and a file input's button and file name, are not measured; text in the field is measured against the field and its ancestors, not against other paint overlapping the field. Under jsdom there is no `::placeholder` style, so placeholders are not measured there; values are. A placeholder below its ratio is a `cantTell` rather than a fail (`PLACEHOLDER_BELOW_THRESHOLD`), whoever chose its colours. WCAG holds placeholder text to the ratio (Understanding 1.4.3), but counts colours the page left entirely to the browser as meeting it (technique G148), and Chromium's own #757575 is 4.61:1 on white, under AAA's 7:1. Telling the browser's colours from the page's on real pages, whose style sheets often come from another origin, is not settled yet, so a person decides for now. Other engines don't measure placeholders.

## Target size

`target-size-minimum` measures the region a pointer can hit, worked out from the layout. A target is its border box with its rounded corners and 2D transforms, clipped by `clip` and `clip-path: inset()` on its containing-block chain and no larger than a box there that clips its `overflow`, with content sticking out of it, less the boxes painted over it that take pointer events, ordered as the contrast rules order paint; it is large enough when a 24 by 24 square aligned to the page fits inside (Understanding 2.5.8). Because the region comes from the layout rather than from hit-testing, targets below the fold are measured as those in view. So are targets in a box that clips its overflow, such as a menu longer than its panel, whether it scrolls with `overflow: auto` or `scroll`, or with `overflow: hidden` and a script (a custom scrollbar, a virtual list): one past the box's edge, or wholly out of view, is measured whole, as scrolled into it, and only the boxes and targets inside the box count as covering it or near it there. Which part of a target shows at the scan's scroll position is never measured. A clipping box only bounds a target's size by its own: a 40px button in a 10px box is measured as 10px. So a target a box half hides for good, with no way to scroll it into view, is measured whole. Rounded corners are measured as drawn; Chromium accepts a pointer about a pixel beyond the curve, so a target measured as a hair under 24 can take a 24 by 24 pointer in that browser. Fixed and sticky boxes over a target are left out, as they cover it at one scroll position only; a target before the start of the page, where scrolling can't reach (a skip link at `left: -9999px`), is no target. Left as their bounding box: 3D transforms, other `clip-path` shapes, and an ancestor's rounded clipping. A square has to fit in one piece of the region (the box, one line of an inline link, one child sticking out), not across two that touch. Its inline exception covers a link in a sentence, which it reads as text with a letter or a digit outside any target in the stretch of the link's block between line breaks; whether such text is a sentence isn't judged, and links between separators alone ("Edit | Delete") that crowd each other are asked about rather than exempted. Under jsdom, which has no layout, the rule is `notApplicable` as before.
